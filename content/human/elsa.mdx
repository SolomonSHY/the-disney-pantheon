Our final pairing is a gift to the reader. If the first thirteen didn't convince
you there's a canonical match between the princesses and the Olympians, the
serendipity of this one surely must: the most famous Greek god who is
*technically* not an Olympian, matched against the most famous Disney heroine who
is *technically* not a princess.

Hades is the god of the dead and the underworld. His realm is cold — a kingdom of
isolation, and it looks like he's the king.

## Hades is Elsa

The eldest male god — the older sibling — is seen as orderly, lawful and fair,
yet feared for his association with death and the underworld. Likewise, Elsa is
the only Disney lead feared for her powers, though she is not evil.

In early drafts of *Frozen,* Elsa was a self-proclaimed Snow Queen with blue
skin, spiky hair and an army of snow monsters, who intentionally froze her
sister's heart. Her concept art bears more than a passing resemblance to Hades as
drawn in Disney's own 1997 *Hercules.*

Hades' most famous item is his Cap of Invisibility (*kyneê*), used in the war
against Cronus. Elsa's childhood revolves around *"conceal, don't feel"* —
manifested by gloves and a locked door. Just as Elsa stops concealing her powers
after becoming queen, Hades stopped using his Cap after becoming King of the
Underworld. And, shared with Demeter, his most important myth is the abduction of
Persephone, an allegory of winter — in which Elsa takes on *all* the major roles
at once: the ruler of the cold realm, the one held captive, and the one who
caused the winter in Arendelle.

---

**Extra-diegetic.** Hades is not an Olympian on a technicality — he doesn't live
on Mount Olympus. His other name is *Plouton,* the Rich One, from all the earth's
underground wealth, which is why the Romans called him Pluto. Elsa, similarly, is
not a Disney princess — because *Frozen* makes so much money that Disney presents
it as its own standalone powerhouse.
