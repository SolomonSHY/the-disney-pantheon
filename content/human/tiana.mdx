Hephaestus is the smithing god. He is also famous for being ugly and lame, and
returned to Olympus only after being exiled for being too unattractive. He is
the god of a transactional marriage — he received Aphrodite in exchange for
Hera's freedom. But most importantly, he is the smithing god. He crafted much
of the magnificent equipment of the gods; almost any finely wrought metalwork
imbued with power in Greek myth is his. It is easier to list the things
Hephaestus *didn't* make, and that is usually only because he wasn't alive yet
to make them.

Did I mention Hephaestus was the smithing god?

So we need a princess who works hard — which already eliminates half the roster.
Preferably she has an actual profession or runs a business. She converts raw
materials into a finished product. And if at all possible, let's have her know
her way around a fire.

## Tiana, from The Princess and the Frog

Her kitchen is his forge. Her pot is his anvil. She runs a successful business.
The Mardi Gras ball is the most Hephaestan image in the canon: the whole
glittering court is in costume, and she is the one holding the tray, making
everything happen. Now let's hammer it in.

- **Work ethic** — Tiana works two waitressing jobs.
- **Transactional relationship** — she trades a kiss to Prince Naveen for a reward.
- **Hideous form** — she turns into a frog. Green and slimy. That form does not appear in the film's promotional material.
- **Cast out** — Tiana is chased out of New Orleans into the bayou.
- **Success through labour** — she builds her dream restaurant by hand.

Most importantly, unlike other princesses whose happily-ever-afters imply a life
of ease, Tiana doesn't stop working even after the credits roll. The forge fires
never dim.

---

**Extra-diegetic.** Hephaestus is the god of blacksmiths and artisans. *The
Princess and the Frog* was Disney's deliberate return to hand-drawn animation
after the studio had closed its 2D division and gone entirely digital. Pencils,
paper, ink — the old craft, revived for this one film.
