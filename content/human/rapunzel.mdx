Apollo has so many titles he could be a one-god pantheon: light, healing,
prophecy, the averting of evil. As god of music and the arts, the lyre is his
major symbol. He healed, yet could also bring plague. He co-invented archery. As
the ideal *kouros* — a beardless, athletic youth — he governs the passage of
children into adulthood. And from the fifth century BCE he was often identified
with Helios, the Sun itself.

So which princess has all of it at once? The answer begins with a drop of
sunlight falling out of the sky.

## Rapunzel

Her powers come from the sun. Her kingdom is Corona, its emblem a golden sun. She
heals by singing — as Apollonic as it gets, for he is the god where medicine and
music are the same act. As a disguise of the god of the arts, she's a polymath:
she reads, paints, plays guitar, knits, cooks, bakes, does pottery,
ventriloquism, ballet, chess. She's the only princess to pick up a stringed
instrument — an essential analogue for Apollo's lyre — and she transforms a tavern
of crooks with a song, drawing each one's hidden artistic dream into the open.

But the strands come together over her defining feature — **hair.** Apollo is
*Phoebus,* brilliant, and *Chrysokomes,* golden-haired. As a *kourotrophos* he
presides over a youth's coming of age, and his Homeric epithet is
*Akersekomes:* he of the unshorn hair — for uncut hair implies eternal youth, and
Greek boys did not cut their hair before puberty. That is Rapunzel's physical
characteristic and her arc in one: brilliant, golden, uncut hair. The film is her
coming-of-age, and the cutting of her hair at the climax is her leaving Mother
Gothel for good.

In case anyone wishes to split hairs, we'll address Apollo's bow. Rapunzel fights
with a cast-iron pan and has no ranged capability whatsoever — but the 2010 film
barely scratched the surface of her powers. In *Tangled: The Series,* three
seasons of Disney canon, her hair grows back indestructible: weapons break on it,
it shields her allies (Apollo *Alexikakos,* averter of evil). And she has three
incantations spanning the god's whole portfolio:

- **Paean,** the healing song we know — heals wounds, reverses aging, restores the recently dead.
- **Smintheus,** the decay incantation — the same melody, different words, draining lifeforce from everything around her.
- **Hekatebolos,** the solar incantation — she levitates, hair and eyes glowing, firing destructive solar bursts that shatter rock.

Ordinarily it's foul play to use future content to reveal a god's abilities. But
Apollo — who gave the gift of prophecy to the Oracle at Delphi, the Sibyls,
Cassandra — is also the god of prophecy. So it's warranted.

is about the god of art and music, made by animators trying to capture light and
