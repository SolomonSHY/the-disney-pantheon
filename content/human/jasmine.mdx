Hera is the queen of heaven, Zeus's wife and sister, goddess of marriage, women
and family. Her temples at Argos and Samos were among the oldest and largest in
the Greek world. Her defining trait in myth is a jealous, vengeful nature toward
any who offend her — especially Zeus's many lovers and illegitimate children.[^protagonist]

[^protagonist]: There is one story where she's the protagonist and comes out
ahead. It features her working her ass off to literally seduce her own husband.

Mapping Hera onto our target princess:

- Hera is the **angry** goddess — the princess must have a temper.
- Direct her rage at the men in her life: give her a catalogue of rejected suitors longer than any other princess.
- Hera's marriage begins with Zeus disguising himself as an injured bird to get close to her — so the princess must have a husband who **lies** to her.
- She should be **regal**, so inured to royalty that she forgets to take off her diadem and gold earrings even in disguise.
- The whole film could be about who gets to marry her, since Hera is goddess of the marriage-bond — but give her the second-lowest screen-time of any princess.
- Her arc is the right to choose, that she isn't a prize to be won. Only — don't actually let her choose. Make her marriage a foregone conclusion the audience saw coming a mile away.

## Hera is obviously Jasmine

Men lie to her for the entire film. Her father, the Sultan, lies about the
immutability of marriage law. Jafar lies as freely as Zeus conducts affairs. And
Aladdin — the *good* one — gets close to her through deception, exactly as Zeus
got to Hera.[^trust] Confronted at the end of the magic-carpet ride, Aladdin has
the perfect chance to come clean, and instead doubles down.

[^trust]: As with many inveterate liars, his signature line is *"Do you trust me?"*

Jasmine is not a woman who distrusts men on principle. She is a woman who has
been *correct.* And that's the tell — because Hera's tragedy is not that she is
suspicious. It's that she is right, every time, about everything, and gets called
shrewish for noticing.

---

**Extra-diegetic.** Hera is defined by not having her own story. Jasmine is the
only Disney princess whose film is named after another character — her lying
husband.
