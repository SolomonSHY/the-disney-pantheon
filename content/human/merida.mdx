Artemis is the Disney princess Merida of *Brave*, and the fit is the tightest in
the matrix. Artemis is the goddess with the bow. Merida is the princess with the
bow. That's really all we needed — but every other aspect of Merida hits the
target too, as though some master archer were using this pair for practice.

**Artemis is the goddess of the wilderness.** Merida's day off is riding into
the highlands alone with a bow, eating an apple on a cliff edge. "Touch the Sky"
is a montage of her choosing wild country over the castle — the waterfall, the
crags, the fire falls she drinks from.

**Artemis is the most virginal of goddesses,** yet her autonomy and ruthless
enforcement of boundaries give her ultimate Chad energy. Faced with a trio of
suitors she is meant to marry, Merida yells, in a Scottish accent, *"I'll be
shooting for my own hand!"* before obliterating the competition — an echo of
Atalanta, Artemis's chosen, who would only wed a man who could beat her in a
footrace.[^atalanta]

[^atalanta]: Which was impossible, unless — like Hippomenes — you happened to
have three golden apples from Aphrodite to use as bait.

**Artemis is a *kourotrophos*,** protector of the young; in some myths she is
born first and immediately midwifes Apollo. Merida is the princess with three
younger brothers — Harris, Hubert and Hamish — and she spends the film looking
after them.

And now the load-bearing part. Bear with me until the facts are laid bare; as we
bear witness, what starts as barely visible quickly becomes unbearable.

- Artemis is associated with wild animals — deer, hunting dogs, and the **bear.**
- One leading etymology derives *Artemis* from Greek *árktos,* "bear."
- She had a bear cult in Attica (Brauronia); the Cave of the Bear Lady on Crete holds Neolithic remains.
- When Callisto, her companion, was seduced by Zeus, an enraged Artemis turned her into a bear — and Callisto and her son Arcas became the constellations Ursa Major and Ursa Minor.

So what does Merida, the wild, prideful archer, do when her mother tries to
force her into marriage? The archetypal teenage act of rebellion: she turns
Elinor into a bear.[^actaeon]

[^actaeon]: Elinor's transformation harks to Artemis's particular cruelty,
decidedly not Disney-friendly: when Actaeon saw her bathing he was turned into a
deer and devoured by his own hounds. Transformation is only the first part of the
sentence — the real horror for Elinor is to be hunted by the husband and
clansmen who love her yet cannot see who she is.

---

**Extra-diegetic.** The original title for Pixar's *Brave* was *The Bear and the
Bow.* And in case the bear-and-bow imagery wasn't enough, they snuck in a final
nod to Artemis, goddess of the moon: in theatres, *Brave* was preceded by a
short film titled *La Luna.*
