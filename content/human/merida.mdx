Artemis is the Disney Princess Merida of Brave, and the fit is the tightest in the
matrix. Artemis is the goddess with the bow. Merida is the Disney princess with
the bow. That's really all we needed to know, but every other aspect of Merida
hits the target, as though some master archer was using this pair as target
practice.

Artemis is the goddess of the wilderness. Merida's day off is riding into the
highlands alone with a bow and eating an apple on a cliff edge. "Touch the Sky" is
a montage of her choosing wild country over the castle — the waterfall, the crags,
the forest, the fire falls she drinks from.

Artemis is the most Virgin of goddesses, but her behavior, autonomy, and ruthless
enforcement of boundaries give her ultimate Chad energy. Merida exemplifies
Artemis's Virgin-Chad duality. Faced with a trio of suitors whom she must marry,
she famously yells in a Scottish accent: *"I'll be shooting for my own hand!"*
before obliterating the competition. This echoes the challenge crafted by
Atalanta, Artemis's chosen, who declared that she would only wed someone who could
beat her in a footrace. Due to Atalanta's notorious speed, this was
impossible.[^atalanta]

[^atalanta]: Unless, as in the case of Hippomenes, you happen to have 3 golden
apples gifted to you by Aphrodite which you can use as bait.

Artemis is a kourotrophos, protector of the young. In some myths, Artemis is born
first and immediately midwifes her brother — she delivers Apollo after Leto's nine
days of labour. Merida is the Disney princess with the 3 younger brothers, Harris,
Hubert and Hamish, is the elder sister of three, and she spends the film looking
after them.

## A load-bearing connection

Finally, we arrive at a load bearing part of the Artemis – Merida connection. Bear
with me here until the facts are laid bare. As we bear witness, what started as
barely visible will quickly become unbearable.

- Artemis is associated with wild animals — deer, her hunting dogs, and the bear.
- One leading etymology derives Artemis from Greek árktos, "bear".
- This theory is supported by the bear cult the goddess had in Attica (Brauronia) and the Neolithic remains at the Arkoudiotissa Cave — The Cave of the Bear Lady — on Crete.
- In some cults Artemis retains the theriomorphic form of a Pre-Greek goddess who was conceived with the shape of a bear.
- When Callisto, a companion of Artemis, was seduced by Zeus, an enraged Artemis turned her into a bear, and in this form she gave birth to her son Arcas, a bear.
- Callisto and Arcas were subsequently placed in the heavens, as the constellations Ursa Major and Ursa Minor (read: Big bear, little bear).

What does Merida, the wild, prideful archer princess do, when her mother tries to
force her into marriage? The archetypal teenage act of rebellion: Merida turns
Elinor into a bear.[^elinor]

[^elinor]: Elinor's transformation into a bear harkens to Artemis's particular
brand of cruelty, which is decidedly not Disney friendly. When the hunter Actaeon
saw her bathing naked, the goddess transformed him into a deer. And then he was
devoured by his own hunting dogs. After Callisto was turned into a bear, she was
nearly killed by her own son. Transformation is just the first part of the
traditional sentence meted out by Artemis. The real horror for Elinor is to be
hunted and killed by her husband Fergus and her clansmen, those who love her but
cannot see who she is.
