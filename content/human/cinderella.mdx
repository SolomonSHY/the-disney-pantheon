Hestia's name means "hearth, fireplace, altar" and she is the virgin Goddess of
the hearth and home. In classical Greek art, she is depicted as a woman simply
and modestly cloaked in a head veil. Hestia is famously dutiful, self effacing
and non-confrontational: she gave up her seat on the 12 Olympians to Dionysus.
And yet, Hestia is considered by some to be the oldest of the Olympians, and the
first recipient of any sacrifice.

After successfully uncovering Zeus, the master of disguise, Hestia serves as a
palate cleanser. We're looking for a princess who works hard around the home —
cleaning, cooking, sweeping. A nonconfrontational princess who serves others and
gets her ass kicked rather than kicks ass. If we were greedy, we'd also want her
name to be associated with the fireplace. And just as Hestia gave up the twelfth
seat on Olympus to Dionysus, the party god, is there a Disney princess famous for
running away from a party at midnight?

## Hestia isn't good at disguises — she's Cinderella

Yet Hestia is a virgin goddess, doesn't that conflict with Cinderella's happily
ever after? No, because Hestia is a virgin by circumstance. Poseidon and Apollo
both wanted her; she swore an oath on Zeus's head to remain unmarried to avoid
the quarrel.

In the film, Cinderella only gets married after the first slipper breaks and she
says: *"That's okay, I have another one."* Cinderella's story shows what might
happen for Hestia should one of her two suitors ever slip out of the race.

**Extra-diegetic.** Hestia is the Goddess of the home and customarily receives
both the first and the last portions of every sacrifice, meal, and libation.
Cinderella Castle is the centrepiece of Walt Disney World and appears in the Walt
Disney Pictures production logo. Every Disney film released for decades opens
with the home of Hestia.
