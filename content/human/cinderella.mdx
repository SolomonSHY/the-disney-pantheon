Hestia's name means "hearth, fireplace, altar," and she is the virgin goddess of
the hearth and home. In classical Greek art she is depicted simply and modestly,
cloaked in a head veil. She is famously dutiful, self-effacing and
non-confrontational — she gave up her seat among the Twelve Olympians to
Dionysus. And yet Hestia is considered by some the oldest of the Olympians, and
the first recipient of any sacrifice.

After uncovering Zeus, the master of disguise, Hestia is a palate cleanser.
We're looking for a princess who works hard around the home — cleaning, cooking,
sweeping. A non-confrontational princess who serves others and gets her ass
kicked rather than kicks ass. If we were greedy, we'd also want her name
associated with the fireplace. And just as Hestia surrendered the twelfth seat
to Dionysus, the party god — is there a princess famous for running away from a
party at midnight?

## Hestia isn't good at disguises — she's Cinderella

Yet Hestia is a virgin goddess. Doesn't that conflict with Cinderella's happily
ever after? No — because Hestia is a virgin *by circumstance.* Poseidon and
Apollo both wanted her; she swore an oath on Zeus's head to remain unmarried, to
avoid the quarrel. In the film, Cinderella only marries after the first slipper
breaks and she says: *"That's okay, I have another one."* Cinderella's story
shows what might happen for Hestia, should one of her two suitors ever slip out
of the race.

**Extra-diegetic.** Hestia is the goddess of the home and customarily receives
both the first and the last portion of every sacrifice, meal, and libation.
Cinderella Castle is the centrepiece of Walt Disney World and appears in the
Walt Disney Pictures production logo. For decades, every Disney film has opened
on the home of Hestia.
