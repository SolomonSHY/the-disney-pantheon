Aphrodite is the goddess of love, lust, beauty, pleasure, passion, procreation.
So we want a princess story with a premise on par with *Too Hot to Handle* or
*Love is Blind.* It might start with a crime — "having no love in their heart" — a
punishment of being turned into something unlovable, and a redemption arc of
loving another and earning their love in return. To spice things up, we'll add a
rival in love, and set a time limit: a rose that will bloom until the eighteenth
birthday.

One movie fits the bill, and don't overthink it.

## Aphrodite is Belle, from Beauty and the Beast

Belle's name is the French word for beauty. She's introduced as the object of
desire by an entire town singing a song literally titled "Belle." She's the only
princess who actually says "I love you." And if that weren't on the nose enough,
her symbols are a magic mirror and an enchanted rose — respectively Aphrodite's
emblem of self-regard and Aphrodite's flower, the rose stained red by her blood
as she ran to a dying lover.

She is the most systematically wooed woman in the canon — a tale as old as time.
Gaston and the Beast court her from the opening scene to the final credits, with
surprises, gifts and dinner. Hers is the only story where fighting over her leads
to the climax.

The savvy reader will spot Aphrodite's own love triangle. The goddess of love
was married to Hephaestus but caught in the act with Ares. **The Beast is
Hephaestus** — the ugly one, the lame one nobody wants to look at, his castle
full of automata (objects he has given life to). Belle gives herself to the
Beast in exchange for her father's freedom, just as Aphrodite was gifted to
Hephaestus in exchange for Hera's freedom. And **Gaston is Ares,** whose response
to a rival in love is to gather an army and march off to war.[^psyche]

"But Belle reads." She does — *far-off places, daring sword-fights, magic spells,
a prince in disguise.* That's not Foucault's Pendulum; that's romance fantasy.
Belle's favourite books are *Twilight* and *Fifty Shades of Grey* — Aphrodite's
calling card.

[^psyche]: The super-savvy reader will note there's an even better fit for this
story: Psyche and Cupid — damning, given that Aphrodite was the jealous
*antagonist* of that tale. Of course, I wouldn't put it past Aphrodite to steal
elements from her rival's love story.
