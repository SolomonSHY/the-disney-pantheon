Hermes is the messenger of the gods, usually depicted with a caduceus — a winged
staff wound with two snakes — his winged sandals (*talaria*) and winged hat
(*petasos*). His breakout role was leading souls to the underworld, and his
repertoire grew to encompass boundaries, travellers, sailors, commerce, and
travel itself. Mediating between opposites — merchant and customer — made him the
god of commerce and of social intercourse.

We want a princess whose story is defined by a boundary between two worlds.
That's easy, so let's tighten it: a story where travel, sailors and commerce are
involved. She mediates between opposites, as guide or translator. As a nod to
Hermes' role as soul-guide, she talks to and receives guidance from spirits. And
she matches his restlessness — she goes wherever the wind takes her.

## That's Pocahontas in a nutshell

The sailors of the Virginia Company — a chartered joint-stock company — have
crossed the Atlantic, the largest boundary to the New World, seeking commerce
and conquest. Two worlds clash, and Pocahontas stands at the divide as guide,
mediator and translator.

What about Hermes' symbols — the sandals, the staff? Pocahontas famously has no
footwear, no item. Or does she?

- **The winged sandals** arrive at the climax: as she runs to stop the execution, she calls on the eagle to help her feet fly.
- **The caduceus** is a length of wood, two serpents, topped with wings — wood, creature, wind fused into one harmonious object. Now take Pocahontas' worldview: the trees are alive, the animals are kin, the wind is full of colour to paint with, and everything is connected.

And for the home run, Hermes' speed. Pocahontas is canonically the fastest
runner in the canon: in the climax she's been clocked at an 8-frame stride —
a sustained 38.2 mph. Usain Bolt tops out at a measly 27.79 mph. And she was
running to deliver a message.

A major objection is her lack of guile. Pocahontas is markedly sombre; Hermes is
the trickster who stole Apollo's cattle on the first day of his life. But look at
her name — not her given name, a nickname she *earned.* In Algonquian,
*Pocahontas* means "little mischief" or "ill-behaved child." Every time someone
says the heroine's name, we're reminded it's Hermes' film. A hidden trick from
the trickster god, hiding in plain sight.
