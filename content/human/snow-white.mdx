Zeus is the canonical master of disguise. No god has disguised himself so often,
or so effectively. To achieve his (usually lascivious) aims he has been a white
bull, an eagle, an ant, a swan, a cuckoo, a serpent, a shower of gold, a dark
mist, a mortal, a goddess, a satyr, a cupbearer. His disguises don't just survive
casual inspection — they pass the most intimate scrutiny.[^semele] So if we don't
see a lightning-wielding, sky-ruling, god-fathering figure among the princesses,
then Zeus is in disguise again, and we can trust it to be a very good one.

[^semele]: He learned this the hard way: revealing his true form incinerated his
lover Semele. Not a mistake he'll make twice.

Where other gods are betrayed by domain or iconography, Zeus's biggest tell is
his **origin story:**

- The titan Cronus learns he is destined to be overthrown by his own child, just as he overthrew his father.
- Cronus demands the infant Zeus; Rhea tricks him, substituting a stone to swallow.
- Zeus is sheltered by the Kouretes, gods of the wild mountainside.
- Zeus returns in disguise and tricks Cronus into drinking a poison, forcing him to disgorge Zeus's siblings.
- After a war, Zeus overthrows Cronus and takes the throne.

There is exactly one princess story that matches this beat for beat:

- An incumbent ruler is threatened by a successor, mirroring how that ruler seized power.
- The ruler tasks an agent to kill the successor, but he loses heart and deceives the ruler with a decoy.[^grimm]
- The successor finds shelter among the wild guardians of the mountains.
- When successor and incumbent finally meet, one is in disguise and one tricks the other into swallowing a poison.
- The incumbent is vanquished; the successor takes the throne.

[^grimm]: In the Brothers Grimm's 1812 *Sneewittchen* the parallel is even more
explicit: the queen eats the decoy lungs and liver with salt, believing them the
girl's.

## The story of Snow White is the Titanomachy, sanitised for Disney

But where's the war, the lightning? Snow White, having just eaten the apple, is
out of action while the dwarfs chase the queen. Or is she? Right as she collapses
and the queen cackles — a flash of lightning, a crack of thunder, torrential
rain. The god of sky and thunder is close at hand. Hounded to a ledge, the queen
might get away, until *crack!* — a **lightning bolt** strikes the rock and she
falls to her death.[^castdown]

[^castdown]: Disney canon states the death was lightning-induced. And note: like
Cronus, the queen isn't merely killed — she is cast *down.*

**Extra-diegetic.** Snow White is the first princess and kickstarted the entire
franchise. To this day, *Snow White and the Seven Dwarfs* (1937) remains the most
successful Disney animated film of all time when adjusted for inflation — an
estimated $2.3 billion worldwide. The king of the gods took the biggest throne.
