Demeter is the Olympian goddess of the harvest and agriculture, presiding over
crops, grains, food, and the fertility of the earth. Her most famous myth
(represented in the Eleusinian Mysteries) is a shared story. Demeter's daughter
Persephone is stolen by Hades, the god of the underworld. Demeter, in her sadness,
neglects to cultivate the earth, plunging the world into a deadly famine where
nothing grows. Only the return of Persephone to her mother alleviates Demeter's grief
and returns life to the world.

Demeter's story is first and foremost an allegory of the seasons, and her primary
narrative role is inaction. We need the princess who appears on screen for about 18
minutes of the 75-minute film,[^screentime] speaks a total of 18 lines, and sleeps
for most of her own movie. And while we're at it, let's make it so that when she
sleeps, the world sleeps with her too.

[^screentime]: Disney princess screen time, ranked —
[GoldDerby](https://www.goldderby.com/gallery/disney-princesses-screen-time).

Demeter is Aurora, also known as Sleeping Beauty.

Other parallels: Aurora is nicknamed Briar Rose, a suspiciously botanical name. She
was protected by three fairies — Flora (Plants), Fauna (Animals) and Merryweather
(Climate), key components of the harvest and agriculture.

Finally, Aurora pricked her finger on the spinning wheel. Now, spinning involves
taking a clump of fibres and teasing a bit of them out, then twisting it into a
basic string shape. What were folks spinning when the *Sleeping Beauty* tale was
first told in 1697? Flax, an agricultural product. When did it spin? After the
harvest, as winter approached.
