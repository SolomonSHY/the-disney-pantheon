Demeter is the Olympian goddess of the harvest and agriculture, presiding over
crops, grains, food, and the fertility of the earth. Though Demeter is often
described simply as the goddess of the harvest, she also presided over the sacred
law and the cycle of life and death.

Demeter's most famous myth (represented in the Eleusinian Mysteries) is a shared
story. Her daughter Persephone is stolen by Hades, the god of the underworld.
Demeter, in her sadness, neglects to cultivate the earth, plunging the world into
a deadly famine where nothing grows. Only the return of Persephone to her mother
alleviates Demeter's grief and returns life to the world. Demeter's story is
first and foremost an allegory of the seasons.

## Demeter is Aurora

Nicknamed Briar Rose, a suspiciously botanical name. Aurora was protected by
three fairies — Flora (Plants), Fauna (Animals) and Merryweather (Climate), key
components of the harvest and agriculture.

Aurora pricked her finger on the spinning wheel. Spinning involves taking a clump
of fibres and teasing a bit of them out, then twisting it into a basic string
shape. What were folks spinning when the *Sleeping Beauty* tale was told in 1697?
Flax. An agricultural product. When were folks spinning it? After the harvest,
just as winter approached.
