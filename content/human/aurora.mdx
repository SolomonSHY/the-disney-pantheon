Demeter is the Olympian goddess of the harvest and agriculture — crops, grain,
food, the fertility of the earth. She also presided over sacred law and the cycle
of life and death. Her most famous myth, dramatised in the Eleusinian Mysteries,
is a shared story: her daughter Persephone is stolen by Hades. In grief, Demeter
neglects the earth, and the world falls into deadly famine; only Persephone's
return restores life. It is, first and foremost, an allegory of the seasons.

Demeter's primary narrative role is her *inaction* — but Disney princesses are
all about action. Except one: the princess who appears on screen for about
eighteen of the film's seventy-five minutes, speaks a total of eighteen lines,
and sleeps for most of her own movie. And while we're at it, let's make it so
that when she sleeps, the world sleeps with her — and the viewers too.

## Demeter is Aurora, nicknamed Briar Rose

A suspiciously botanical name. Aurora is protected by three fairies — Flora
(plants), Fauna (animals) and Merryweather (climate) — the key components of
harvest and agriculture. She pricks her finger on a spinning wheel. Spinning
takes a clump of fibres and twists them into string. What were folks spinning
when the *Sleeping Beauty* tale was told in 1697? Flax — an agricultural product.
And *when* were they spinning it? After the harvest, as winter approached.

**Extra-diegetic.** The original *Sleeping Beauty* (1959) was a box-office bomb.
Many at the animation studio were laid off. This led to a thirty-year winter
before Disney had a successful princess film again — its own Demeter famine.
