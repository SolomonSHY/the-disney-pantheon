Dionysus is the god of wine-making, orchards, fruit, vegetation, fertility,
festivity, insanity, ritual madness, religious ecstasy, and theatre. Unlike
Artemis, who rules the wilds, Dionysus specifically exists to subvert authority and
liberate his followers from social convention.

In Greek mythology, Dionysus is a god of foreign origin — the arriving
outsider-god. Ariel exhibits obsessive xenophilia through the treasures she has
collected. Ashore, Ariel is the perfect embodiment of the stranger. No one knows
where she came from, who she is, why she is in Prince Eric's kingdom or how she got
there.

Dionysus is the god of theatre, madness and ecstasy. Ariel has to mime theatrically
to get any message across. She's bewilderingly unfamiliar with local
customs.[^customs] She seizes the reins during the carriage ride and drives at
reckless speed, the exact shape of Dionysus.

[^customs]: Mistaking a fancy porcelain finger bowl filled with warm water for a
soup or tea cup, she raises it up and takes a confident drink from it, much to the
confusion of Grimsby. She believes a common fork is used to style hair. She
believes a smoking pipe is a musical instrument.

In the Orphic myth, Dionysus' old body was destroyed and he needed to be born in a
new one. Ariel ends her film in a new body — on two legs.

But where's the wine? Dionysus is the god of wine. No wine, no Dionysus. I don't
care if it's a G-rated Disney movie and all the official Disney princesses are
underage.[^underage]

[^underage]: Megara from Hercules is the only heroine presented drinking something
that might be alcoholic.

Where would the wine be? Dionysus' wine, music, and ecstatic dance were considered
to free his followers from self-conscious fear and care, and could ease suffering,
bring joy, and inspire divine madness. So it must be at the point of Ariel's
transformation.

Let's zoom in on that scene. It's a clandestine meeting frowned upon by any right
thinking merfolk. Ariel is here on impulse, in direct defiance of King Triton, which
sets the scene perfectly. The festivals of Dionysus are subversive and transgressed
traditional social and moral constraints.

Something's brewing. Literally, as Ursula throws magical ingredients into a
bubbling, steaming cauldron — a concoction for poor unfortunate souls who are
miserable, lonely and depressed. That's basically saying that it's drugs or alcohol.
It promises Ariel happiness and freedom… at a cost. She surrenders the organ of
self-expression — her voice. And it isn't just self-expression: her voice is how
Prince Eric recognizes her so she's also trading her identity. For dionysian ecstasy
dissolves the boundary of the self.

The scene escalates. There's smoke and flashes of light, like fireworks at a
festival. Ursula's singing and dancing. Ariel sings. Now Ursula laughs madly. Drink
the magic juice! But she doesn't! In the Disney movie, smoke leaps from the cauldron
and seizes Ariel, completing the transformation. What is so obviously a brew doesn't
even act like a liquid, or go anywhere near her mouth.

To actually find the brew, I had to go to a story written in 1837 by Hans Christian
Andersen, *Den lille Havfrue,* and found this banger of a line: *"Den lille havfrue
drak den brændende skarpe drik, og det var, som gik et tveægget sværd igennem hendes
fine legeme, hun besvimede derved og lå, som død."*

Or, in English: *"The little mermaid swallowed the bitter, fiery draught, and it was
as if a two-edged sword struck through her frail body; she swooned away and lay
there as if dead."*

There's the wine. And it is strong stuff. One might almost say it is 100% proof.

mother, was incinerated by Zeus's true form while pregnant. To save the unborn
fetus, Zeus retrieved the baby from Semele's ashes and sewed Dionysus into his own
thigh until the child was ready to be born. This granted Dionysus the epithet "the
twice-born god". Now, by 1985 Disney animation was effectively dead. *The Black
Cauldron* had bombed so badly the studio considered shuttering the animation
division entirely. No Disney princess had appeared in thirty years, since Aurora in
1959. The lights were going out. Then in 1989 a girl with a stolen voice came up out
of the water, kickstarted with an injection of Broadway theatre talent, and the
studio was born a second time. *The Little Mermaid* signaled the beginning of the
decade-long Disney Renaissance, during which the animation studio was born
again.[^renaissance]
