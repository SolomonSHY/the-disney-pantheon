The algorithm has three main limitations.

## It can't factor in how good a god is at disguises

The rubric doesn't account for how well each god hides. This bites a little with
the masked tricksters, Dionysus and Hermes, but it *fell apart* with Zeus. Fable
was utterly fooled by his disguise, found no satisfying match except Jasmine —
and in doing so accidentally kicked out the right answer, Hera. To uncover Zeus's
identity you have to discount iconography, temperament and domain entirely, and
look purely at narrative role and story arc.

## It doesn't map antagonist gods well

Fable struggled to place the villainous Olympians — Poseidon, Ares and Hera, and
to a lesser degree Aphrodite and Artemis. Their antagonistic personalities don't
fit the princess persona, so the model rated their matches harshly, never asking
how such a god would *translate* onto a Disney heroine. For these gods you have
to upweight iconography, domain and story arc, and downweight narrative role and
temperament.

## It doesn't use the film's gestalt

Fable had trouble identifying what a Disney film is all *about.* It missed that
Belle's film is about love, that Ares's film is about war, and that Dionysus's
film is about… well, whatever Dionysus is about. It muddled its way through
because usually the film's theme leaks into the rubric, but a human would have
named the meta-narrative and been far more confident.

The clearest symptom was Aurora and her eighteen minutes of screen time. A human
viewer identifies her story as an allegory of winter, recognises Demeter's story
as an allegory of winter, and connects the dots. Instead the model took
Demeter's themes of blight and restoration and mapped them onto Moana and Raya —
where blight and restoration are the plot drivers, but not the *theme.*

The common thread: when it met a foreseeable difficulty — a god who is a master
of disguise, a god who always loses, a princess who sleeps through her own film —
Fable stuck to its matrix and couldn't make the creative correction.
