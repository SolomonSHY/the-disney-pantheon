Here, at the very bottom of the scale, the whole apparatus admits its limits. Snow
White scores barely seven against Zeus — the weakest fit in the canon, and an authorial
override at that. No weighting of this rubric produces the pairing. The editor
simply insisted on it, and the honest thing is to say why the insistence fails.

Zeus is sovereignty: the sky-father, king of the gods, thunder and rule and
patriarchal command. Snow White is a fourteen-year-old who keeps house for seven
miners and is kind to birds. The only thread connecting them is that both sit,
formally, at the top of a hierarchy — she is *first* of the princesses, the 1937
original, the sovereign of the line by seniority if nothing else. It is a claim
about film history, not about character.

> Her true analogue is Persephone — the girl taken by death and returned — and
> Persephone is not an Olympian this pantheon can seat.

So read this chapter as the method's confession. The facets are nearly all cold;
the arc is empty; the temperament barely registers against a thunder-god. Snow
White defeats death by lying in a glass coffin until love arrives — a resurrection
myth wearing a crown that doesn't fit. Sometimes the most useful cell in a matrix
is the one that proves the matrix has an edge.
