Raya is the newest heroine here and the most armoured. She is a lone warrior in a
broken world, a guardian of the last shard of a talisman, hardened by a betrayal
she cannot forgive. Her film is a war story about whether a soldier can put the
sword down — which makes her a study in the god of war, examined from the inside.

Ares is not the honourable choice; that is the point. He is violence itself,
courage curdled toward aggression, the god nobody prays to fondly. Raya is what
Ares looks like when the story refuses to let him win: all that guardianship and
courage and readiness to fight, held under a discipline that keeps asking whether
the fighting is worth it.

This was not the algorithm's first choice for her, and the score — a hard-won
twenty-seven — shows the friction. War and guardianship pull the pairing up;
Ares's raw brutality pulls it back down, because Raya's courage is finally in
service of *trust*, the one thing the god of war has never understood. She is
Ares learning the lesson that would stop him being Ares.
