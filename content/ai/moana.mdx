Moana is chosen by the ocean before she is chosen by the plot. The sea is a
character in her film — it parts for her, returns her fallen necklace, carries and
tests her. She is a wayfinder, daughter of navigators, whose entire arc is a
voyage across open water to set right a wound in the world. Her domain is, simply,
the sea.

Poseidon owns that domain outright: god of the ocean, of storms and navigation,
the earth-shaker whose moods are the water's moods. On raw territory the match is
obvious, and it very nearly topped her row.

But look at temperament, and the pairing strains. Poseidon is wrathful,
vengeful, a drowner of sailors; Moana is the ocean's collaborator, not its
tyrant. So the score settles in the low twenties: a total agreement on *where*
and a real disagreement on *who*. She commands the same sea Poseidon does, in a
completely opposite key — which is why the ocean, in her film, is finally gentle.
It found someone it didn't need to punish.
