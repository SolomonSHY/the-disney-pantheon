Everything about Rapunzel is *light*. Her hair is spun gold that glows when she
sings; it heals wounds and reverses age; the whole plot turns on a single day of
lanterns rising like a second sunrise. She is a solar body kept locked in a
tower, and her power is the one Apollo owns.

Apollo is the god of the sun and of healing — the two gifts braided into
Rapunzel's hair — and also of music and of youth, the bright god whose songs mend
what they touch. Rapunzel heals by singing. That is almost a direct translation
of his cult: light, health, and melody as a single continuous power.

The pairing lands in the mid-twenties because Apollo is an enormous, sprawling
deity — prophecy, plague, archery, the lyre — and Rapunzel occupies only his
warmest quarter. She is Apollo the healer and the sun, with none of Apollo the
punisher. The domain and iconography glow; the rest of his vast portfolio simply
doesn't apply to a girl whose worst crime was wanting to see the lights.
