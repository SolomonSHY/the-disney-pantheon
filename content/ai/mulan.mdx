Mulan wins her war by thinking. The avalanche that buries the Hun army is not
strength but a single well-aimed rocket and a calculated angle — strategy
defeating force, which is the oldest lesson Athena teaches. She is a soldier who
succeeds precisely because she is *disguised*, an infiltrator inside her own
army, cunning where the men around her are merely brave.

Athena is the war-goddess who despises Ares. Where he is slaughter, she is
tactics, the patron of heroes and the mind behind the shield. She is also,
pointedly, the virgin goddess who steps outside the marriage plot entirely — and
Mulan's film opens by rejecting the matchmaker and closes without surrendering to
a wedding it could easily have staged.

> Strategy, discipline, disguise, and a refusal to be married off on schedule.
> The overlap is nearly a definition.

The high marks land on role and temperament — mentor's discipline, the
strategist's cunning — and domain follows close behind through war-craft and
wisdom held together. What keeps her a hair below Cinderella is iconography:
Athena's owl, aegis, and spear are a fixed heraldry, while Mulan's emblems keep
changing as her disguise does. Even her symbols are tactical.
