No princess in the canon has hands like Tiana's. She is the only one whose dream
is a *building* — a thing of brick and copper and rendered fat, raised by labour
rather than granted by rescue. Where the others wait, wish, or are chosen, she
works a double shift and saves the tips in a cocoa tin. That is not a romance.
That is a forge.

So the arithmetic barely hesitates. Against Hephaestus — the lame smith of
Olympus, the only god who sweats, the maker who is loved for what he builds and
not for how he looks — Tiana scores the single highest cell in the entire matrix.
Domain, arc, and temperament all peg the ceiling: the god of *making* meets the
princess who makes.

> He is the god the others laugh at until they need a shield. She is the woman
> the city ignores until it needs somewhere to dance.

The one soft facet is iconography. Hephaestus is hammer, anvil, and volcanic
fire; Tiana is a gumbo pot and a waitress's apron. Different tools, same covenant
with work. The symbols diverge precisely because the labour is so completely
shared that it needs no matching emblem.

This is what a maximal fit looks like: not a costume resemblance, but two figures
who believe the same thing about effort and earn it the same way.
