Aurora sleeps for most of her own film. She is cursed at birth, hidden in the
forest, and then unconscious through the climax — a princess whose defining act is
*dormancy*, the world holding its breath around a girl who has withdrawn from it.
There is no god of sleep on the roster, so the pairing has to be argued rather
than measured.

The editor's choice is Demeter, and the argument runs through Persephone. Demeter
is the goddess of the seasons and the grain, whose grief for a daughter taken
below turns the world to winter — and whose daughter's return is spring. Aurora is
that suspended earth: the kingdom frozen in enchanted sleep, waiting for the
season to turn and the sleeper to wake. Read her as the winter Demeter causes, and
the myth clicks a quarter-turn into place.

But be honest about the number. This is an authorial override, and the score —
not even twelve — is the lowest fit the editor was willing to defend. Aurora's
entire row spans barely ten points; *no* god fits her, because a heroine who
spends her third act asleep offers almost nothing for the facets to grip. The
machine ranked Demeter fourth. The editor promoted her anyway, on the strength of
a season the film never quite shows.
