Pocahontas lives on a threshold — between two peoples who cannot understand each
other, and she is the only one who can cross. She learns a language "with all the
colours of the wind," carries messages between camps, and stops a war by stepping
bodily onto the boundary line at the moment of execution. Her gift is
translation, and translation is a god's job.

Hermes is that god: the crosser of borders, patron of travellers and
interpreters, the messenger who moves between worlds — Olympus and earth, the
living and the dead — because he belongs fully to neither. Where others hold a
domain, Hermes holds the *space between* domains. So does she.

> The interpreter is never quite trusted by either side. That distrust is the
> price of standing in the doorway.

The score cools here, into the high twenties, and honestly. Hermes is also the
trickster and the thief — mischief Pocahontas never touches — so the temperament
match is partial. What holds the pairing together is role and domain: the
messenger, the boundary-walker, the one who interprets. She is the diplomat half
of a god who is also a scoundrel.
