Aphrodite is the goddess of love, lust, beauty, pleasure, passion, procreation. We
need a Disney princess story with a premise on par with Netflix hit reality TV
series *Too Hot to Handle,* *Single's Inferno,* or *Love is Blind.*

The tale might start with a crime: 'having no love in their heart', a punishment:
'being turned into something unlovable', and resolve with redemption: 'loving
another and earning their love in return'. To spice things up, we'll add a rival in
love, and also set a time limit gimmick: 'a rose that will bloom until the 18th
birthday'.

One Disney princess movie fits the bill, and don't overthink it.[^disguises]
Aphrodite is Belle from Disney's Beauty and the Beast.

[^disguises]: Aphrodite's disguises are famously ineffective. In Book III of
Homer's Iliad, Aphrodite disguises herself as an old wool-comber to fetch Helen of
Troy, but her divine identity is immediately given away by her beautiful neck,
perfect breasts, and flashing eyes.

Belle's name is the French word for beauty. Belle's symbols are a magic mirror and
an enchanted rose, which are, respectively, Aphrodite's emblem of self-regard and
Aphrodite's flower — the rose that in myth is stained red by her blood as she runs
to a dying lover. She is the most systematically wooed woman in the canon and the
only princess where men fighting over her led to the climax. And if that wasn't on
the nose enough for you, she's the only Disney princess who actually says "I love
you".[^iloveyou]

[^iloveyou]: With the exception of Ariel, who says "I love you, Daddy" to King
Triton and Rapunzel who says "I love you more" to Mother Gothel. For obvious
reasons, they don't count.

The mythologically inclined may realize that Belle's story is Aphrodite's canonical
love triangle. The Goddess of Love was married to Hephaestus, yet caught cheating
with Ares, the god of war. The hideous beast represents Hephaestus. His workshop is
famously full of automata: golden handmaidens who move and think and speak, objects
he has given life to. And Gaston is Ares, whose response to a rival in love is to
gather an army and march to war.[^psyche]

[^psyche]: The extra mythologically inclined reader might realize there's an even
better fit for Beauty and the Beasts's story: that of Psyche and Cupid. Aphrodite,
being a jealous goddess, probably plagiarised elements from her rival's love story.

"But Belle reads." She does… Far-off places, magic spells, and a disguised Prince
Charming for the female protagonist to discover. That is not Foucault's Pendulum or
the Brothers Kamarazov, that's romance fantasy. Belle's favorite books are *Fifty
Shades of Grey* and *Twilight* — Aphrodite's calling card.

---

**Extra-diegetic.** In the Judgement of Paris, Aphrodite famously edged out Hera
and Athena for the golden apple inscribed "to the fairest". Beauty and the Beast is
the most critically acclaimed film in the Disney princess canon, and it made
history as the first animated movie ever nominated for the Academy Award for Best
Picture[^bestpicture] — the only princess film ever nominated at all. The goddess
of beauty takes the apple again.
